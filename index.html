Ph.D. Student in Information Systems

I study how digital platforms and AI systems affect individuals' behavior, judgments, and well-being. My work combines controlled behavioral experiments with causal inference using large-scale platform and field data to examine both the mechanisms underlying these effects and their behavioral consequences.

About

I am a Ph.D. student in Information Systems at the Kelley School of Business, Indiana University. My research focuses on two areas: how people respond to AI-mediated interactions and how platform and incentive design affects user behavior. I use behavioral experiments and causal inference with large-scale platform and field data to study these questions.

Before starting my Ph.D., I completed an M.S. in Information Systems and a B.S. in Business Administration at Kyung Hee University in Seoul.

See the Research page for my current projects in Behavioral Information Systems and Economics of Information Systems / Empirical IS, and the CV page for my academic record.
