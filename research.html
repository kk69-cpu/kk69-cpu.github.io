Research

My work studies how digital platforms and AI systems reshape the behavior, judgments, and well-being of the people who use them. I pursue this through two connected streams: controlled experiments that isolate the psychological mechanism behind people's response to AI-mediated interaction, and causal inference on large-scale platform and field data that measures the real behavioral and economic consequences of platform design choices.

Human-AI Interaction

Working Paper

Authenticity in the Age of AI: A Multidimensional Perspective on AI-Generated Products

K. P. Kang, A. B. Brendel, A. R. Dennis

When and through which dimensions of authenticity does AI involvement in product creation influence purchase intention, and how does the knowledge characteristic of a product category shape these effects? A 3×4 between-subjects experiment (N = 621) crossing AI involvement (disclosed via a label modeled on the EU AI Act) with four product knowledge types finds that AI involvement broadly reduces perceived authenticity, but that the specific authenticity dimension driving purchase intention, and whether AI's involvement significantly damages it, depends on the product category. Target: MIS Quarterly. This is also my first-year Ph.D. milestone paper.

Working Paper

When Multi-Agent Interviews Help or Hurt: Design Principles for Agentic Interview Systems

L. Wang, K. P. Kang, H. Bala

Which multi-agent design choices, such as coordination among AI agents and role specialization, improve or harm the quality of insights elicited from participants during an AI-led interview, and through what mechanisms (cognitive workload, interviewer credibility, social presence)? IRB-approved two-study experimental design, with a fully deployed production research platform. Target: Information Systems Research.

Digital Platforms and User Behavior

Submitted · Management Science

Penalty Scarring: The Hidden Cost of Loss-Incorporating Incentive Designs on Digital Platforms

S. W. Jin, K. P. Kang, K. S. Kang, J. H. Park

Digital platforms widely use loss-incorporating incentive designs, such as streaks, deposits, and earned status, that are valued for the effort they elicit before failure. Using a staggered difference-in-differences design on a deposit-based commitment platform, we find that achievement rates fall by roughly seven percentage points after a user's first loss (penalty scarring), a decline that is not reduced by user-side adjustments and that grows larger with the size of the loss. Loss activation roughly doubles the likelihood of platform exit. Currently submitted to Management Science. This paper's lineage traces back to my M.S. thesis at Kyung Hee University and five earlier conference presentations (SCECR/WITS/INFORMS 2022, INFORMS 2024, HICSS 2025).

Working Paper

How Social Media Shapes Personality Development in Young People with ADHD: Evidence from Reddit

K. P. Kang, R. Kan, L. Yan

Using a large-scale dataset from the r/ADHD community (642,000+ posts, 6.1 million comments) and personality-trait scores extracted by fine-tuned language models, this project asks whether self-disclosure of identity information relates to subsequent change in users' measured Big Five personality traits and to their continued engagement on the platform. Analysis is progressing from descriptive comparisons toward panel fixed-effects regression, event-study designs, and survival models of platform retention. Target: Information Systems Research.

Working Paper

AI-Inferred Personalization in Digital Retail: How LLM-Generated Inference Shapes Consumer Trust, Privacy Concerns, and Purchase Behavior

K. P. Kang, J. Tan, M. Santa, H. Bala

Conducted in partnership with a real grocery-delivery e-commerce company, this project distinguishes preference inference (what products an AI predicts you want) from identity inference (who an AI infers you are), testing how disclosure of the AI's reasoning shapes consumer trust and purchase behavior. The research design pairs a lab and online experiment with a randomized field experiment on the partner platform's real customers. Target: Management Science.
