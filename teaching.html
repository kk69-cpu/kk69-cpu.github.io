Teaching

I believe students learn analytical methods more effectively when they understand the problems those methods are designed to solve. My teaching focuses on three principles: connecting methods to concrete problems, understanding how data are generated, and interpreting the practical implications of analytical results.

Teaching Philosophy

Connect methods to concrete problems. I learned analytic methods through research and industry projects: improving donor contributions in a crowdfunding campaign, verifying wheel-design similarity for an automotive manufacturer, and evaluating the incentive design of a mobile health application. Each project had a concrete problem that required a specific analytical method. I use the same approach in teaching: connecting methods to the problems they are designed to address.

Understand how data are generated. My research involves platform secondary data, corporate datasets, and human-subjects data collected through experiments. These experiences have taught me that analyzing data requires understanding how the data were generated and what they measure. This understanding is necessary to determine which conclusions the data can and cannot support.

Connect analysis to decisions. Analytical results become useful when students can interpret their implications. I want students to move beyond producing statistical results or model outputs and explain what the results mean, how they address the original problem, and what limitations should be considered when making decisions based on them.
